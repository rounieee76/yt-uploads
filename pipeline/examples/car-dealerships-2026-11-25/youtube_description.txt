On the night of June 18th, 2024, hackers knocked out the software behind about 15,000 car dealerships. Six months later, the FTC and Illinois reached a $20 million settlement with a Chicago-area dealer group, the FTC's largest ever with a car dealer. In September 2025, Tricolor, a Dallas used-car dealer and lender, collapsed into Chapter 7. And on October 1st, 2026, California gave used-car buyers three days to cancel.

Yet the average new car now sells for a record $51,109. And the dealer's profit on that car has collapsed.

So what really happened? This video follows the money: the markup years, why the finance office and the service bay now earn more than the car itself, what one extra point on your loan costs, and who is paying the bill now.
