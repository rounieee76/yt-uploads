You're a baby on the bank of the Onon River, and in your tiny fist you're holding a clot of blood. At nine, your father is poisoned by his enemies. Then your own clan rides away and leaves your family to starve. You're hunted, caught, and dragged from tent to tent with a wooden board locked around your neck. Your wife is kidnapped. Your blood brother turns on you. And the man you call father tries to have you killed. This is the whole life of Genghis Khan, from a boy named Temujin to the khan whose empire became the largest connected land empire in history, told as if it were yours.

The blood clot, the poisoned meal, the fish and Begter, the wooden collar, Borte, Jamukha, the muddy water of Baljuna, the nine-tailed banner, the caravan at Otrar, and a grave nobody has ever found.
